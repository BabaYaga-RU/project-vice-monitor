Rockstar Re-Engineered Weather for Leonida

Grand Theft Auto 6 will feature a significantly upgraded weather system, according to a GameSpot report citing comments from Rockstar's vice president of art for lighting and rendering, Owen Shepherd, in an interview with Game Informer. Shepherd said the studio has "re-engineered" its weather technology to "make everything more localized and persistent," aiming to give GTA 6's Florida-inspired setting of Leonida a sense of scale the developer has never achieved before.

Per the report, the game will include "fully dynamic cloudscapes that roll across the map" delivering a "broad spectrum of weather." Given Florida's reputation for heat, humidity, and fast-moving localized rain, GTA 6 is designed to replicate that: looking one direction might reveal bright sunshine while the other shows a downpour, with localized rain showers and rainbows appearing. Areas where it recently rained will stay wet, with puddles forming.

Wind Systems, Storms, and Unknown Gameplay Effects

GameSpot reports that GTA 6 also introduces a new wind system featuring "offshore breezes after sunset, convectional updrafts, and occlusion from buildings." Shepherd described visible effects: "Hair and debris will swirl around on rooftops; gusts of wind will blow ripples over the water surface and bend the grass in swamps."

The game will also include tropical storms and hurricanes, along with thunder and lightning. However, Shepherd did not detail how these weather systems might affect gameplay, and it remains unknown whether events like a hurricane could knock down structures or trees. GTA 6 launches on November 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, priced at $80 for the standard edition and $100 for the Ultimate Edition, with a $400 collector's edition that includes physical items but not the game.
